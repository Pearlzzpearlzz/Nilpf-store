04 – House Rules and Community Standards

Shared Housing Program

PURPOSE

This document outlines general house rules and community standards intended to support a safe, respectful, and cooperative shared living environment.

These standards apply to all participants equally and are not services, supervision, or program rules.

SHARED LIVING EXPECTATIONS

shared housing program is based on mutual respect and personal responsibility.

Participants are expected to:

  • Respect the rights, privacy, and personal space of others
  • Maintain reasonable cleanliness of shared areas
  • Use shared spaces cooperatively and responsibly
  • Communicate respectfully with other participants

No participant is responsible for managing, supervising, or correcting another participant.

PERSONAL RESPONSIBILITY

Each participant is responsible for:

  • Their own behavior, choices, and actions
  • Their own guests, belongings, and conduct
  • Compliance with applicable laws and housing terms

The housing provider does not supervise daily activities or interpersonal interactions.

SAFETY AND CONDUCT

Participants agree to:

  • Refrain from violence, threats, harassment, or intimidation
  • Avoid activities that create unsafe or hazardous conditions
  • Respect quiet hours and shared living norms
  • Follow basic fire safety and emergency guidelines

Illegal activity is not permitted within the housing environment.

SHARED AREAS AND PROPERTY

Participants are expected to:

  • Use shared furnishings and facilities with care
  • Clean up after themselves in common areas
  • Avoid damaging property or belongings

Damage caused by a participant may result in financial responsibility in accordance with housing terms.

GUESTS

Guests must be respectful of the shared living environment and comply with house standards.

Participants remain responsible for the conduct of their guests at all times.

Additional guest guidelines may be outlined in a separate Guest Addendum.

NO SUPERVISION OR ENFORCEMENT ROLE

House rules and community standards do not create a supervisory or enforcement role.

The housing provider does not monitor behavior, impose treatment requirements, or manage interpersonal conflicts beyond basic housing-related matters.
